# Health

It is important to maintain a healthy flock in order to ensure safety for your chickens, your family, and public health. Chickens can not only get sick themselves, but may also spread disease to other animals and people. Below are resources to help guide maintaining healthy backyard chickens.

## Preventative care

It is often said "an ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure", and that is true for backyard chickens as well. The more diseases you prevent in your flock, the less you have to treat! A good place to start is having good biosecurity practices.

### Biosecurity

##### What is biosecurity?

Biosecurity is the process of implementing a practice of keeping infectious viruses/bacterias away from the flock as well as keeping the flock away from viruses/bacterias and other health hazards [[C1](/citations?id=citation-c1)]

##### How do I implement biosecurity at my house for my flock?

* Keep visitors to a minimum
* Limit the number of people you allow into your coop and handling your birds
* Wash your hands before and after contacting the flock
* Keep shoes/ boots clean
* Use disposable boot covers, disinfectant foot baths, or both before and after entering the coop
* Ideal to designate boots for inside your coop/ around your flock
* Quarantine chickens that show signs of sickness OR any new additions to your flock [[C1](/citations?id=citation-c1)]

## Common health issues

Just like people, chickens can suffer from various health issues. Trauma from animal attack, reproductive diseases (egg binding - stuck in the reproductive tract, low calcium, cancer), respiratory diseases (bacterial, viral, fungal) and bumblefoot (foot inflammation/infection) are commonly cited reasons chickens see a veterinarian [[C4](/citations?id=citation-c4)].

### Monitoring your chicken's health

Healthy birds are active, curious, have open eyes, and hold their head up [[C5](/citations?id=citation-c5)]. Make sure to observe your chickens from far away as well as up close, because sometimes sick birds pretend to not be sick in order to avoid perceived predators [[C5](/citations?id=citation-c5)].

## Veterinary Services

### When to seek veterinary care

Any chicken that is not showing signs of good health, has their head consistently down, eyes closed, is having difficulty breathing, or is lying down and unresponsive should seek veterinary care.

### Where to seek veterinary care

While some veterinary clinics will see chicken patients, others may not. If you have a certain clinic nearby that is convenient for you, make sure to call ahead and check that the clinic does see chicken patients. The Association of Avian Veterinarians may also be a good place to start in order to inquire or seek veterinary care for your backyard chickens.
